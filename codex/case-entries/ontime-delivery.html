The Situation

A 18-person creative agency delivering across multiple concurrent projects. The team was experienced and capable. Work was consistently being delivered — just not on time and not to a consistent standard.

Deadlines were treated as targets rather than commitments. When something ran over, it was absorbed rather than escalated. Senior staff were regularly pulled into fixing work that should never have reached them. And clients were following up on project status before the agency did — which is the clearest possible signal that the delivery system is not running.

The Diagnosis

The agency had no defined delivery gates — no checkpoints at which work was formally reviewed before moving to the next stage. Work moved forward by momentum rather than milestone. When something was wrong, it was usually discovered at the end rather than the beginning.

Ownership at handoff points was assumed rather than assigned. When work passed between team members or departments, nobody formally accepted it. Gaps in quality or timing between handoffs were nobody's responsibility — so they were the founder's.

What Was Built
  • ✦Delivery gates at defined stages of each project — work could not progress to the next stage without a formal checkpoint. Senior review moved from the end to the middle of the process.
  • ✦Handoff design with explicit ownership transfer — work was formally handed and formally accepted. Gaps between stages had named owners.
  • ✦Definition of Done for each deliverable type — a written standard that removed ambiguity about what completed work looked like.
  • ✦Weekly delivery cadence — a structured review of all active projects, surfacing at-risk items before they became late items.
