The Situation

A bootstrapped B2B SaaS business where every decision — regardless of size, urgency, or complexity — eventually found its way back to the founder. Department leads existed in title. In practice, they functioned as senior contributors who escalated decisions upward rather than making them.

The founder was not micromanaging. The team was not incapable. The system had simply never been built. Nobody had ever told the team what they were authorised to decide — so the default was always to ask.

The Diagnosis

Two things were missing. A decision-rights framework that defined authority by role and threshold. And a cultural permission — explicit, documented, communicated — that the team did not need to wait.

The founder was the operating system. Not because they wanted to be, but because nothing else had been built to replace that function. Every path led to their Slack and stayed there.

What Was Built
  • ✦A Decision Rights Framework defining decision authority at each level of the organisation. What required founder involvement and what did not — written down for the first time and shared with the whole team.
  • ✦An async Intake → RACI → Closeout flow for work requests — removing the informal channels that had always defaulted to the founder.
  • ✦An accountability cadence that tracked decision outcomes by owner — making it visible when decisions were being made at the right level and when they weren't.
