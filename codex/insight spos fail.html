Insight

SOPs don't fail because they're wrong. They fail because nobody uses them.

The document isn't the problem. The system around the document is. Here's what makes the difference.

Most businesses that have SOPs have the same experience with them. Someone writes them — usually after something breaks, usually by someone senior who knows how things should work. They get filed somewhere. People are told they exist. And then gradually, quietly, they stop being used. A few months later someone does a task incorrectly and the post-mortem reveals that there was an SOP for it that nobody had read.

The instinct is to blame the people. They should have followed the process. But that's rarely the real problem. The real problem is that the SOP was written to be stored, not to be used. It was written by someone senior who knew the process already, for people who needed to understand it for the first time. And it was filed somewhere that made sense to the person who filed it, not to the person who needed to find it.

An SOP that nobody uses isn't a process. It's documentation of how things were supposed to work — which is a completely different thing.

There are three things that determine whether an SOP gets used. First — is it findable? Not just technically accessible but genuinely easy to locate when someone is in the middle of doing a task. Second — is it written for the person doing the work, not the person who knows the work? SOPs written by experts for experts are impenetrable to anyone learning the task for the first time. Third — is it maintained? A document that was accurate six months ago and hasn't been touched since is not a living process. It's a historical record.

The fix is a single-source library that is designed to be used, written for the person doing the task, and reviewed on a defined cycle. Not filed. Used.

This applies when
  • ✦You have SOPs but quality still varies depending on who's doing the work
  • ✦New hires ask questions that documented processes should answer
  • ✦Processes were written by someone who has since left and nobody is sure if they're current
  • ✦Something broke and the investigation revealed there was a documented process that wasn't followed
